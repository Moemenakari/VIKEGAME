# History Bible — the sources behind Jarlheim

Jarlheim's mechanics come from the mobile 4X genre. Its **world, names, offices,
laws, festivals, heroes and monsters** come from medieval Norse texts and
archaeology. The Norse told history and legend side by side in their sagas, and
so do we. Every historical element in the game should trace back to a row here.

> All of these texts are medieval and long in the public domain. Their
> characters and stories are free to use. We write our own text and never copy
> modern translations word for word, since translations can still be under copyright.

## 1. Primary written sources

| Source | Date / author | What we take from it |
|---|---|---|
| **Heimskringla** | c. 1230, Snorri Sturluson | Kings' sagas: royal offices (*stallari*, *ármaðr*, *merkismaðr*), the beacon-fire warning system of Hákon the Good, Olaf Tryggvason's *Long Serpent* (built by the master shipwright Þorbergr Skafhögg), the Battle of Svolder (Einar Thambarskelfir's bow), Harald Hardrada and the Varangian Guard |
| **Prose Edda** (*Gylfaginning*, *Skáldskaparmál*) | c. 1220, Snorri Sturluson | Cosmology: the Nine Worlds, Jörmungandr, Thor's fishing trip, the wolves Sköll & Hati, Ragnarök. Skaldic poetry |
| **Poetic Edda** (Codex Regius) | 13th c. manuscript of older poems | *Hávamál* (stanzas 76–77: "fame never dies" is our tagline), *Hymiskviða* (Thor fishes for the serpent), *Völuspá* (the seeress's prophecy, Ragnarök) |
| **Landnámabók** (Book of Settlements) | 12th–13th c. | **Land-taking**: settlers threw their high-seat pillars (*öndvegissúlur*) into the sea and settled where they washed ashore (Ingólfr Arnarson). A man could claim the land he could **circle carrying fire in one day**, and a woman the land she could lead a heifer around from sunrise to sunset. Aud the Deep-Minded |
| **Íslendingabók** | c. 1125, Ari Þorgilsson | Founding of the **Althing** (c. 930), the **Lawspeaker** (*lögsögumaðr*) reciting the law at the **Law Rock** (*Lögberg*) |
| **Grágás** (Icelandic law codes) | 12th–13th c. | **Full outlawry** (*skóggangr*) vs **lesser outlawry** (*fjörbaugsgarðr*), compensation payments, legal procedure at the Thing |
| **Egils saga** | 13th c. | Egil Skallagrímsson, warrior-poet. He raised a **níðstang** (nithing pole, a curse pole) against King Eric Bloodaxe and Queen Gunnhild |
| **Njáls saga** | 13th c. | **Blood feud & wergild**: killings answered by revenge or settled by compensation at the Thing |
| **Grettis saga** | 14th c. | Grettir the Strong defeats **Glámr the draugr**, our main undead model |
| **Eyrbyggja saga** | 13th c. | Restless dead (Þórólfr Lame-foot), hauntings, more draugr lore |
| **Laxdæla saga** | 13th c. | Aud the Deep-Minded, feasts, chieftain politics |
| **Saga of the Greenlanders & Erik the Red's Saga** | 13th c. | Erik the Red (Greenland), Leif Erikson and Thorfinn Karlsefni (**Vínland**), Freydís Eiríksdóttir |
| **Orkneyinga saga** | c. 1200 | Jarls of Orkney: a jarldom as a power base |
| **Jómsvíkinga saga** | 13th c. | The Jomsvikings, a sworn warrior brotherhood with strict laws (model for clan rules) |
| **Völsunga saga** | 13th c. | Sigurd slays the dragon **Fáfnir** and wins the cursed hoard |
| **Hervarar saga** | 13th c. | Shieldmaiden Hervor and the cursed sword **Tyrfing** |
| **Ragnars saga loðbrókar** | 13th c. | Ragnar Lodbrok (legendary) and his sons |
| **Gesta Danorum** | c. 1200, Saxo Grammaticus | **Lagertha** and the shieldmaidens (Book IX) |
| **Konungs skuggsjá** (King's Mirror) | c. 1250 | The **hafgufa**, a giant sea monster mistaken for an island: our "giant fish" event boss |
| **Risāla of Ahmad ibn Faḍlān** | 921–922 | An Abbasid envoy's eyewitness account of the **Rūs** on the Volga: their dress, trade, and a ship funeral. The link between the Norse world and the Arab world |
| **Gesta Hammaburgensis** | c. 1075, Adam of Bremen | Uppsala, Birka, northern trade |
| **Anglo-Saxon Chronicle** | 9th–12th c. | Raids on England (from 793), the Great Heathen Army (865) |
| **Russian Primary Chronicle** | c. 1113 | Rurik, the Varangians, the route "from the Varangians to the Greeks" |
| **Rune poems** (Norwegian, Icelandic) | 12th–15th c. | Names and meanings of the **16 runes of the Younger Futhark**, the structure of our research tree |

## 2. Archaeology (for visuals and buildings)

| Site / find | Use in game |
|---|---|
| **Oseberg & Gokstad ships** (Norway) | Longship models: proportions, carved dragon prows |
| **Skuldelev ships** (Roskilde) | Five real ship types, from the small warship to the ocean-going *knarr* cargo ship: our ship classes |
| **Trelleborg ring fortresses** (Denmark, c. 980, Harald Bluetooth) | The **Ring Fort** wall and the circular stronghold layout on the world map |
| **Birka, Hedeby, Kaupang, Dublin** | Trading towns (*kaupangr*): Silver Road destinations |
| **Spillings Hoard** (Gotland, about 67 kg of silver, many Arab dirhams) | Hacksilver and dirham-hoard visuals. Silver is valued **by weight** (scales in the UI) |
| **Birka grave Bj 581** (genomic study, 2017: a high-status warrior grave belonging to a woman) | Archaeological support for our shieldmaiden heroes |
| **L'Anse aux Meadows** (Newfoundland) | The Norse site in North America: the far-west **Vínland** region |
| **Jelling stones** (Denmark) | Runestone art style for the **Runestone Field** |
| **Ship settings** (Lindholm Høje, Denmark; Anundshög, Sweden): graves marked by standing stones in the outline of a ship | The **Draugr Barrow** model |
| *Skjaldborg* (shield-fort, the shieldwall) in the sagas and skaldic verse | The Shield Yard's Norse name and the Shieldwall troop |
| *Bóndi*, *karl*, *húskarl*: free farmer, free man, a lord's sworn household warrior | Shieldwall tier names |
| **Longhouses** (Borg in Lofoten, reconstructed halls) | The **Great Hall** with its high seat, hearth and benches |
| **Hjallr (stockfish drying racks)**, Lofoten | Fish production building |
| **Útibúr** (raised storehouses on posts) | The Storehouse model |
| **Rauðablástr** ("red-blowing": smelting bog ore, *rauði*, in clay shaft furnaces) | The Bog-iron Smelter model and name |
| Old Norse **skáli** (hall), **bær** (farm), **viðr** (wood), **kaupangr** (market town) | Building names in the game |
| **Bog-iron smelting sites** | The **Bog-iron Smelter**: Norse iron came from bog ore |

## 3. Things we deliberately leave out
- **Slavery** (thralls were real, but we leave them out).
- Real religious sites as raid targets. Monsters and rivals are the enemies.
- Romanticised "horned helmets": there is no evidence the Vikings wore them in battle, so our helmets are rounded and spectacled, like the **Gjermundbu helmet**.

## 4. Rule for contributors
Before adding a name, office, festival or creature, add or cite its row here.
Legendary elements (dragons, draugr, giants) are fine because they come from the
Norse texts themselves. Mark them *legend* in the GDD.
